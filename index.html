I study international macroeconomics and monetary policy in small open economies: the financial frictions that give exchange-rate policy a role, and the nominal rigidities through which external shocks reach domestic prices. My work combines quantitative models with granular data, including the daily item-level price database I built at the central bank.

At the BCRP I produce the macroeconomic projections and risk scenarios behind the monetary policy decision. I hold an MSc in Economics (Distinction) from the London School of Economics.

Exchange rate dynamics and policy

What Does FX Intervention Buy? A Quantitative Evaluation of Peru's Managed Float
Working paper2026 · Draft available on request

I evaluate the BCRP's foreign exchange intervention in the Itskhoki–Mukhin (2026) model, in which intermediaries charge a state-dependent premium for holding currency risk, solved globally and calibrated to Peru over 2002–2025. I quantify how much of Peru's exchange-rate volatility is financially driven and how much of the welfare gain from optimal intervention a simple rule delivers, both on average and during the 2020–2021 stress episode, and compare the rule with the BCRP's observed intervention.

Discusión de teoría de política de intervención cambiaria y hechos estilizados para Perú
[FX intervention policy: theory and stylized facts for Peru]
Policy articleRevista Moneda, submitted

A review of how the segmented-markets literature resolved the puzzle that sterilized intervention should be irrelevant, organized around four policy questions (when intervention works, which shocks it should answer, what it should stabilize, and the role of reserves), with Peruvian data to read the BCRP's practice.

Resumen (ES)

La intervención cambiaria es práctica habitual entre los bancos centrales de economías emergentes, pero el modelo canónico predice que una intervención esterilizada no debería tener efecto alguno sobre el tipo de cambio. Este artículo revisa la literatura que resolvió esa tensión incorporando mercados financieros segmentados, y ordena las reglas de política que de allí se derivan en torno a cuatro preguntas: cuándo es efectiva la intervención, frente a qué choques conviene usarla, qué debe estabilizar y cuál es el rol de las reservas. Con datos del Perú, ofrece claves para interpretar la práctica del BCRP a la luz de estos marcos.

Price setting and inflation

Do Firms Adjust Prices through Frequency or Magnitude? Granular High-Frequency Evidence from Peru's Inflationary Episode (2020–2024)
Working paper2025 · Draft available on request

Using supermarket microdata through Peru's 2020–2024 inflation, I show that firms responded by changing prices more often, not by changing them more: the hazard of adjustment rose while the average size of price changes stayed flat. The evidence points to state-dependent rather than time-dependent pricing.

Abstract

This paper analyzes firm-level price adjustment behavior during Peru's inflationary episode from 2020 to 2024, using high-frequency data from supermarket products. Guided by a simple Rotemberg (1982) model, I distinguish between two margins of adjustment: the frequency and the magnitude of price changes. After filtering out discounts and short-lived price spells, I document a clear upward shift in the distribution of year-on-year price changes, particularly for food items, confirming widespread price adjustment. The price change distribution is skewed toward positive values and bimodal, with few small changes, indicating that firms are more likely to implement sizable price increases than marginal adjustments, in line with the presence of adjustment costs. Hazard rate estimates show that half of prices change within two months, and that the likelihood of price adjustments rose during the inflationary period. Finally, I find that rising inflation was associated with an increase in the frequency of price changes, while the average magnitude remained stable. These results suggest that firms primarily respond to inflation by adjusting prices more often, rather than by raising them more sharply.

From Daily Prices to Inflation Trends: Constructing a Daily Consumer Price Index for Peru
Working paper2025 · Draft available on request

Daily price indexes built from web-scraped data follow official inflation trends but reveal what monthly aggregates hide: raw price dispersion is twice the official estimate, and commodity shocks reach their peak effect on processed-food prices 200 to 300 days later.

Abstract

We investigate price dynamics using web-scraped data from supermarkets. We constructed daily price indexes and compared them with official monthly inflation data and studied their reaction to commodity price shocks. To remove noise caused by short-lived price changes, a discount filter was applied to the price series. We compared four characteristics of the official statistics. First, the online price indexes show higher magnitudes of year-on-year variations than official estimates, but follow similar trends. Second, the trends of both price indexes seem to align in the long term, but indexes built out of individual prices seem to deviate for longer periods. Third, the ratio of food to non-food goods prices estimated with online data aligns with official inflation trends but diverges in magnitude for certain periods. Finally, the dispersion of raw prices is twice the estimate in official sources. Nonetheless, we show that aggregation captures relative price adjustments and ignores inner dynamics of price-setting, like coordination. Furthermore, we estimated impulse-response functions to commodity price shocks using a local projection specification. We found that the effect on processed foods tends to peak between 200 and 300 days after a shock. These findings highlight the potential of high-frequency data as an early indicator of inflation trends.

Inflation and Price Dynamics Analysis using High-Frequency Data from Supermarkets: Evidence from Peru
PublishedIFC Bulletin 66, Bank for International Settlements, 2026 · Presented at the 12th Biennial IFC Conference, Basel

Builds the BCRP's first daily item-level price database, scraped from supermarket websites and classified into COICOP categories. Online price changes peaked later than official inflation but fell faster, and price durations shortened as inflation rose.

Abstract

I analyzed daily supermarket price data from Peru spanning the period of inflation increase from 2020 to 2022, followed by its subsequent reduction from 2023 and early 2024. The Central Reserve Bank of Peru lacked high-frequency price indices to monitor inflation and understand price formation. To address this gap, daily data from web pages of supermarkets was extracted and classified according to the COICOP classification. The findings reveal that price changes peaked later than official data but declined more rapidly. Price changes increased as inflation grew. This resulted in a negative correlation between inflation and duration of prices, which could be regarded as a shift in the inflation regime. Changes seem to be balanced between positive and negative values or biased towards the positive ones, so that variations related to trend prices are larger for increases than decreases.

Patrones de precios de alimentos procesados
[Price patterns of processed foods]
Publishedwith L. Olivera · Revista Moneda 196, pp. 45–55, BCRP, 2023

Using daily supermarket price data from Peru, we identify five patterns of processed-food pricing based on discount-driven volatility and the frequency of regular-price adjustments. The analysis reveals differences across products and package sizes, highlighting how temporary discounts can obscure underlying price trends and how high-frequency data can improve inflation monitoring.

Resumen (ES)

Este artículo realiza una primera exploración a la base de datos de precios de supermercados y clasifica el comportamiento de los alimentos procesados a partir de criterios sobre su volatilidad y frecuencia de actualización.
